## Executive Summary

This briefing examines claims surrounding emerging non-local weapons technologies allegedly predicated on concepts such as the **Aharonov–Bohm (A-B) effect**, the **Dynamical Casimir Effect**, and **gravito-electromagnetics**. These ideas are often described in speculative literature as involving pulsed, frequency-modulated magnetic fields interacting with biological systems.



Key claims described in such discussions include:

* **Medical Correlation:** Reports of neurological symptoms in incidents sometimes referred to as *“Havana Syndrome.”*
* **Mechanism of Action:** Hypothesized interaction between electromagnetic fields and neural microstructures such as microtubules.
* **Physics of Entanglement:** Claims of non-local correlations between emitters and biological targets.
* **Technical Parameters:** Descriptions emphasizing timing patterns or field modulation rather than high energy levels.

# I. Medical Profiling: Havana Syndrome and Neurological Pathology

Incidents beginning in **2016 involving diplomats in Havana** led to investigations into unexplained neurological symptoms. Similar reports later appeared in other locations.

## Reported Symptoms

Individuals reporting exposure described symptoms including:

* Balance problems and vertigo  
* Tinnitus or unusual auditory sensations  
* Headaches and nausea  
* Hearing loss  
* Memory and concentration difficulties  

## Neuroimaging Findings

Some studies examining affected individuals reported differences in brain imaging compared with control groups.

Examples reported in literature include:

* **White and Gray Matter Differences**  
  Reduction in certain regional brain volumes.

* **Microstructural Integrity Changes**  
  Alterations observed in cerebellar tissue.

* **Functional Connectivity Changes**  
  Differences in auditory and visual-spatial network connectivity.

### Regions Discussed in Studies

* Right thalamus and ventral diencephalon  
* Auditory cortex (superior temporal gyrus, Heschl’s gyrus)  
* Visual-spatial areas including frontal gyrus and cuneus  
* Inferior parietal sulcus  
* Insula  

Interpretations of these findings remain debated within the scientific and medical communities.

Certain speculative models propose interactions between electromagnetic fields and neural microstructures.

Microtubules are structural components within cells and play roles in:

Some theoretical frameworks suggest they could have additional computational or quantum properties, although this remains an active and controversial area of research.

# IV. Physics Concepts Referenced in Discussions

Several physical phenomena are often cited in discussions of hypothetical non-local technologies.

## Aharonov–Bohm Effect

The **Aharonov–Bohm effect** demonstrates that electromagnetic potentials can influence quantum particles even in regions where electric and magnetic fields are zero.

This is a well-established quantum mechanical phenomenon, although its application to biological or macroscopic systems remains speculative.

## Angular Momentum and Spin

In quantum physics:

* **Spin angular momentum** describes intrinsic particle rotation.
* **Orbital angular momentum** describes motion around a point.

These properties are fundamental in quantum systems but have **no demonstrated mechanism for macroscopic remote influence of biological systems**.

## Poynting Vector

The **Poynting vector** describes the directional energy flow of an electromagnetic field.

It is defined as:

\[
S = E \times H
\]

where:

* \(E\) is the electric field  
* \(H\) is the magnetic field  

This concept is widely used in electromagnetics and antenna engineering.

## Gravito-Electromagnetics

Gravito-electromagnetics is a theoretical analogy between gravity and electromagnetism used in certain general relativity models.

Its practical engineering applications remain limited.

These properties are studied primarily in:

# VI. Timing and Frequency Effects

Some reports emphasize the role of signal timing and frequency in biological interaction with electromagnetic fields.

### Biological Rhythm Frequencies

Human brain activity includes several common frequency bands:

| Frequency Range | Associated Brain State |
|-----------------|-----------------------|
| Delta (0.5–4 Hz) | Deep sleep |
| Theta (4–8 Hz) | Relaxation / memory processing |
| Alpha (8–12 Hz) | Calm wakefulness |
| Beta (13–30 Hz) | Active thinking |
| Gamma (>30 Hz) | High cognitive processing |

These rhythms are widely studied in neuroscience.

### Magnetic Stimulation Techniques

Two known research techniques include:

* **Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS)**  
  Uses strong magnetic pulses to stimulate neurons.

* **Weak-field stimulation research**  
  Investigates lower-intensity electromagnetic fields interacting with neural rhythms.

These techniques are studied in medical contexts such as depression treatment and neuroscience research.
