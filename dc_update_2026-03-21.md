## Deep Dive: AI and War in Iran

Strategic Integration of Artificial Intelligence in U.S. Military Operations: The Project Maven Transition

Executive Summary

The United States Department of Defense (DoD) is undergoing a fundamental shift in its strategic approach to warfare, establishing artificial intelligence (AI) as a cornerstone of its "kill chain" operations. Central to this transition is the designation of Palantir’s Maven Smart System as an official "program of record." This move, articulated by Deputy Secretary of Defense Steve Feinberg, ensures long-term funding and military-wide adoption of weapons-targeting AI that has already been deployed extensively in the conflict with Iran.

The military's "speed wins" doctrine has seen the aerial targeting cycle compressed from weeks or days to mere seconds. While AI has enabled the processing of vast data sets that previously overwhelmed human analysts—striking approximately 7,000 targets in three weeks—the transition has raised significant ethical and legal concerns. Notable incidents, such as the strike on a school in Minab Province, highlight the risks associated with rapid, AI-enabled decision-making and the reliance on out-of-date intelligence within automated systems.

The Institutionalization of Project Maven

According to a March 9, 2025, memo from Deputy Secretary of Defense Steve Feinberg, the Pentagon is moving to lock in the use of Palantir’s AI across all military domains. This institutionalization marks a significant victory for Palantir and a shift in how the DoD manages its digital assets.

Key Organizational Changes

* Program of Record Status: Designation as a program of record provides Maven with stable, long-term funding and streamlines its integration across the Joint Force.
* Oversight Relocation: Management of the program is moving from the National Geospatial Intelligence Agency to the Pentagon’s Chief Digital Artificial Intelligence Office (CDAO).
* Contracting Authority: Future contracts with Palantir regarding Maven will be handled by the U.S. Army.
* Implementation Timeline: The decision is expected to be fully in effect by the close of the current fiscal year in September 2026.

Strategic Rationale

The Pentagon views AI-enabled decision-making as essential to "detect, deter, and dominate" adversaries. The current volume of intelligence—derived from satellites, drones, radars, and signals—is described as a "noise" that humans can no longer process alone. AI is used to automatically detect, classify, and label military assets, such as enemy vehicles and weapons stockpiles, turning data analysis into a process of seconds rather than hours.

The AI-Enhanced Kill Chain (F2T2EA)

The "kill chain" refers to the end-to-end targeting sequence. AI has been deeply embedded in this process to accelerate every stage, particularly during the ongoing conflict with Iran.

Stage	Action	AI Role & Impact
Find	Intel gathering	Synthesizes Human (HUMINT), Imagery (IMINT), and Signals (SIGINT) data simultaneously.
Fix	Pinpoint location	Compresses hours of cross-referencing into seconds to provide precise GPS coordinates.
Target	Evaluation	Conducts Collateral Damage Estimates (CDE) by calculating blast radii against "pattern of life" data.
Track	Monitoring	Predicts the movement of mobile targets (Time-Sensitive Targets) to facilitate future strikes.
Engage	Execution	Generates Courses of Action (COA) to recommend the best asset to prosecute a target.
Assess	Review	Conducts real-time Bomb Damage Assessment (BDA) to determine if a re-strike is necessary.

Operational Efficiency and Scale

The deployment of AI has fundamentally altered the scale of military operations and the personnel required to sustain them.

* Target Volume: The U.S. military reported striking approximately 7,000 targets within the first three weeks of the war with Iran.
* Personnel Reduction: Investigation by Georgetown University found that the U.S. Army’s 18th Airborne Corps used AI to reduce an intelligence processing team from 2,000 members to just 20.
* Data Sifting: AI can cross-reference millions of data points, including thousands of satellite passes and hundreds of hours of drone footage, in seconds.

Critical Risks and Complications

Despite the operational advantages, the rapid adoption of AI in targeting has introduced severe technical and ethical challenges.

The Minab Province Incident

The strike on a school in Iran’s Minab Province, which killed 175 people (mostly schoolgirls), serves as a primary example of system failure.

* Outdated Intelligence: Preliminary investigations suggest the strike used out-of-date coordinates.
* Failure of "Pattern of Life": While AI is intended to flag civilian areas (schools, hospitals) using "pattern of life" monitoring, this system failed despite satellite imagery from December 2025 showing children in the courtyard.
* Human Error vs. AI Safeguards: Experts suggest that a properly programmed AI should have flagged the entity as a school using available Google map sources and imagery, potentially correcting human errors in the targeting loop.

Technical Supply Chain Risks

A significant complication in the adoption of Maven is its reliance on the Claude AI tool, developed by Anthropic.

* Security Concerns: Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth ordered Claude stripped from Pentagon systems just before the war started, citing supply chain risks and a "spat" over safety guardrails.
* Integration Bottleneck: Despite the order, Claude remains the "backbone" of Palantir’s Maven targeting system. Experts suggest it is impossible to "rip out" such an integrated component overnight without a viable replacement.

Legal and Ethical Warnings

United Nations expert panels have warned that AI weapons targeting raises significant risks regarding inadvertent bias in training data. While Palantir maintains that humans remain responsible for the final lethal decision, the speed of the AI-driven kill chain places immense pressure on human operators to approve recommendations generated in seconds.

The Pentagon’s AI strategy acknowledges these tensions but concludes that the "risks of not moving fast enough outweigh the risks of imperfect alignment."
